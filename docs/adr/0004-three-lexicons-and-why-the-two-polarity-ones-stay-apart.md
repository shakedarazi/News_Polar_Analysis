# Three lexicons, and why the two polarity ones stay apart

The repo has three word lists. Two of them produce a number called a "ratio" over
comment text, and nothing in the code says which is which. This records the split
before a second polarity score exists to be confused with the first.

## What each list is

| file | entries | runs on | measures | column |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `data/lexicon_base/category1..7.txt` | 329 | article text | **topic** — what the article is about | `windows_features.c1..c7`, `dominance` |
| `data/comment_lexicon_base/polar_words.txt` | 182 | comment text | polarity, one axis | `comments_features.polar_ratio` → `audience_mean` |
| `data/lexicon/polarization.csv` | 191 | comment text | polarity, **two axes** | new; this ADR |

The first one is not a competitor to the other two. It answers "which of seven
subjects does this sentence belong to", and `dominance` is how concentrated a
window is on its top subject. A window full of political vocabulary scores high
whether the article is calm or furious. It measures topic, never heat.

The other two both measure polarity, both over comments, and both were written by
someone as "the polar words". That is the collision this ADR exists for.

## Why the research lexicon is not a replacement

`polarization.csv` is the Hebrew adaptation of Simchon, Brady & Van Bavel (2022),
"Troll and divide: the language of online polarization", PNAS Nexus 1(1) pgac019.
Its dictionary was not hand-written — it was derived from which words travel only
inside one political camp — and its hierarchical clustering split in two:
`issue` (what the argument is about) and `affective` (hostility toward the other
side). 191 lemmas: 122 affective, 69 issue, each with a provenance label
(110 simchon, 34 media-v2, 11 israeli-supplement, 36 ai-review).

Swapping it in for `polar_words.txt` would be a silent reinterpretation of every
number already stored. Measured over the expanded forms both lists actually match
against:

```
polar_words.txt expanded      2,394 surface forms
polarization.csv expanded     2,480 surface forms  (1,583 affective, 897 issue)
shared                          372  (15%)
only in polar_words           2,022
only in polarization          2,108
```

`audience_mean` on every analyzed article is built on those 2,022 forms. A
replacement does not refine that number, it invalidates it — while keeping the
same column name, the same range, and the same place in the UI.

So the research reading is stored **alongside** as `issue_count` /
`affective_count` and their article-level means, and `polar_ratio` /
`audience_mean` keep meaning exactly what they have always meant.

## The rule

**The two polarity readings are never summed, averaged, or substituted for each
other, and never presented as one number.** They disagree on 85% of their
vocabulary, so any blend is a third measurement that no research backs.

Where both appear, each says which list it counted.

## Matching stays offline-expanded

`src/lexicon/deterministic_matcher.py` matched tokens by stripping Hebrew
prefixes *and suffixes* (`יות ות ים ין ה י ו ת נו כם ן`) recursively at runtime.
That contradicts the invariant the rest of the pipeline is built on — lexicon
matching is a lookup against a set expanded once at build time, never runtime
stemming — and its over-matching was never measured against this corpus.

The expansion path (`expand_lexicon`, 12 prefix forms, minimum base length 3) is
what the exhibit's on-screen numbers were computed with, so keeping it also means
the site and `exhibit-2026-09` can be checked against each other. Measured on
this lexicon, the cross-axis collision path in `_insert_surface` never fires:
zero surface forms are generated by both an `issue` and an `affective` lemma.

The matcher, `src/features/` (a 2022 vertical slice from `919beff` that was never
wired into any pipeline), and `scripts/process_one_article.py` are removed.

## Consequences

- Two polarity columns per comment instead of one. `NULL`, not `0`, until scored —
  "not measured" and "measured as zero" are different, as with `dominance`.
- `--polarization-only` on `analyze_articles.py`, mirroring `--windows-only`, so a
  backfill over ~50k stored comments does not re-run window extraction.
- Anything comparing sources or events must state which list it counted. Two
  rankings that disagree are the expected result, not a bug.
